01 · Where it started

It started with people close to me.

I watched my mom and relatives in their middle age struggle with menopause.

02 · Understand

10 interviews. Three ways of facing it.

3 perimenopause · 5 postmenopause · 2 surgical — one hour each, plus 4 existing apps. Symptoms differ for every woman, so I looked at how they cope.

The endurer

"It's natural — just let it pass."

The pre-preparer

Already looked after her health before menopause, often with a doctor.

The self-explorer
Target user
  • Searches, asks and watches herself to find what works
  • Prefers lifestyle change before medicine
  • Keeps her own notes of what helps and what triggers symptoms
03 · Define

The hard part is the trial and error.

Unexpected symptoms→ Realize menopause→ Seek info / support⇄Adapt / treat→ Accept→ Renew
How might we create an empowering, personalized tool for women who want to adjust and explore their unpredictable changes — with a sense of independence and control?
04 · Ideate

A sandbox to try plans, not a rulebook.

S
Self-independence

Choices to adapt through behavior changes and experiments, not only medicine.

H
Helping hand

Baseline, personalized information as a guide — content checked with a doctor.

E
Empowerment

She can take control of unpredictable days, in her own style of self-care.

06 · Solution

Log it, try a plan, see if it works.

Swift journalLog a sudden symptom in seconds.
My planPick a suggestion or make your own, with reminders.
Symptom recordCompare plans against severity over time.
ArchiveKnowledge plus recipes and reviews from other women.

07 · Result & next

What testers said, and what I'd change.

The record is really useful — my friend and I write on paper, and it's gone when we need it.
It helps me tell the doctor my symptom history when I can't cope anymore.
It follows how women in menopause turn information into their own wisdom.
